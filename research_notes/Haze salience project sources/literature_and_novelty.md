## 2. Gradual information diffusion and underreaction

### Takeaway
The theoretical anchor is Hong-Stein (1999) and the empirical "slow diffusion across linked assets" literature (Cohen-Frazzini, Menzly-Ozbas, Hong-Torous-Valkanov, Da-Gurun-Warachka). Two user-supplied attributions need correcting: Hirshleifer-Peng-Wang is in the Review of Financial Studies (2025), not JF; the common-ownership JFE paper is Ying (2024).

### Cited Findings
- Hong, Harrison and Jeremy C. Stein (1999), "A Unified Theory of Underreaction, Momentum Trading, and Overreaction in Asset Markets", Journal of Finance 54(6): 2143-2184. DOI 10.1111/0022-1082.00184. Model: gradual diffusion among "newswatchers" produces underreaction then momentum traders cause overreaction. [M, not re-checked; reliable memory]
- Hong, Harrison, Terence Lim and Jeremy C. Stein (2000), "Bad News Travels Slowly: Size, Analyst Coverage, and the Profitability of Momentum Strategies", Journal of Finance 55(1): 265-295. DOI 10.1111/0022-1082.00206. Momentum stronger in low-coverage stocks, especially for losers. [M]
- Hong, Harrison, Walter Torous and Rossen Valkanov (2007), "Do Industries Lead Stock Markets?", Journal of Financial Economics 83(2): 367-396. DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2005.09.010. Industry returns predict market returns, consistent with gradual diffusion. [M]
- Cohen, Lauren and Andrea Frazzini (2008), "Economic Links and Predictable Returns", Journal of Finance 63(4): 1977-2011. DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2008.01379.x. Customer-supplier links: customer news predicts supplier returns, evidence of limited attention. [M]
- Menzly, Lior and Oguzhan Ozbas (2010), "Market Segmentation and Cross-predictability of Returns", Journal of Finance 65(4): 1555-1580. DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01578.x. Upstream/downstream industry returns cross-predict, stronger where investors are less informed. [M]
- Da, Zhi, Umit G. Gurun and Mitch Warachka (2014), "Frog in the Pan: Continuous Information and Momentum", Review of Financial Studies 27(7): 2171-2218. DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhu003. Momentum stronger when information arrives in many small increments than in a few large jumps (limited attention to gradual news). Search to verify returned nothing relevant; the venue/year are from memory. [M] — directly relevant: continuous fire accumulation vs a salient haze "jump".
- Anton, Miguel and Christopher Polk (2014), "Connected Stocks", Journal of Finance 69(3): 1099-1127. Common mutual-fund ownership links predict return comovement; cross-stock reversal strategy. Venue/year confirmed in a secondary search summary. [V-partial] — [LSE copy](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/ConnectedStocks.pdf)
- Ying, Jie (2024), "Gradual information diffusion across commonly owned firms", Journal of Financial Economics 156 (article 103852). DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2024.103852. Common institutional ownership slows cross-firm information spread; monthly cross-predictability; CIO peer-momentum strategy beats shared-analyst momentum; strongest for passive, low-turnover owners. Details from abstract/metadata; page numbers not captured, full text not read. This is the "common-ownership paper in JFE" the brief asks about (author identified by search; confirm no other JFE candidate). [V-partial] — [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v156y2024ics0304405x24000758.html); [Brattle summary](https://www.brattle.com/insights-events/publications/gradual-information-diffusion-across-commonly-owned-firms/)
- Hirshleifer, David, Lin Peng and Qiguang Wang (2025), "News Diffusion in Social Networks and Stock Market Reactions", Review of Financial Studies 38(3): 883-937 (NOT Journal of Finance; no JF version found). NBER Working Paper 30860 (2023); earlier title "Social Networks and Market Reactions to Earnings News". Finding: higher county social-network centrality -> stronger immediate reaction to earnings news, weaker drift, but more persistent volume ("social churning"). [V] — [NBER w30860](https://www.nber.org/papers/w30860); [IDEAS RFS record](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v38y2025i3p883-937..html)
- Lopez-Lira, Alejandro and Yuehua Tang, "Can ChatGPT Forecast Stock Price Movements? Return Predictability and Large Language Models", arXiv:2304.07619 (v1 15 Apr 2023; v6 28 Oct 2025); also on SSRN. No peer-reviewed journal publication found as of this search. LLM scores of headlines positively predict next-day returns, stronger for small stocks and negative news, consistent with limits to arbitrage. Treat as a working paper. [V-partial: arXiv listing] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2304.07619)
- Semenova, Valentina and Julian Winkler (2025), "Social contagion and asset prices: Reddit's self-organized bull runs", Quantitative Finance 25(12): 1873-1904 (NOT Review of Finance). Earlier title "Reddit's self-organised bull runs" (arXiv:2104.01847; MPRA). WSB attention is self-exciting; 2SLS link to trading volume. Venue/pages come from a single search summary. [V-partial] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2104.01847); [Oxford INET](https://www.inet.ox.ac.uk/publications/social-contagion-and-asset-prices-reddits-self-organised-bull-runs)

### Inferences
- Most apt for the project's hypothesis: Hong-Stein (newswatcher diffusion), Da-Gurun-Warachka (continuous small signals under-attended), Cohen-Frazzini (attention-driven cross-asset lag). Fire hotspots accumulate continuously, matching the "frog in the pan" structure.
- Lopez-Lira-Tang and Semenova-Winkler are peripheral (LLM/social media); cite sparingly.

### Gaps
- DOIs/pages for items marked [M] not resolved. Hirshleifer-Peng-Wang DOI not captured.

## 3. Limited attention and salience

### Takeaway
Huberman-Regev (2001) is the closest analogue (old public information repriced after press coverage). The rest supply the attention-proxy and salience toolkit.

### Cited Findings
- Huberman, Gur and Tomer Regev (2001), "Contagious Speculation and a Cure for Cancer: A Nonevent that Made Stock Prices Soar", Journal of Finance 56(1): 387-396. DOI 10.1111/0022-1082.00330. EntreMed soared after a Sunday NYT article on research already published in Nature and the press five months earlier; spillover to other biotechs; permanent rise. [V] — [EconPapers](https://econpapers.repec.org/RePEc:bla:jfinan:v:56:y:2001:i:1:p:387-396); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v56y2001i1p387-396.html)
- Barber, Brad M. and Terrance Odean (2008), "All That Glitters: The Effect of Attention and News on the Buying Behavior of Individual and Institutional Investors", Review of Financial Studies 21(2): 785-818. DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhm079. Individuals are net buyers of attention-grabbing stocks (news, extreme volume/returns). [M]
- DellaVigna, Stefano and Joshua M. Pollet (2009), "Investor Inattention and Friday Earnings Announcements", Journal of Finance 64(2): 709-749. DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01447.x. Weaker immediate reaction and stronger drift when announced on low-attention Fridays. [M]
- Hirshleifer, David, Sonya S. Lim and Siew Hong Teoh (2009), "Driven to Distraction: Extraneous Events and Underreaction to Earnings News", Journal of Finance 64(5): 2289-2325. DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2009.01501.x. More same-day announcements -> weaker reaction, larger drift. [M]
- Da, Zhi, Joseph Engelberg and Pengjie Gao (2011), "In Search of Attention", Journal of Finance 66(5): 1461-1499. DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2011.01679.x. Google Search Volume as direct attention proxy (usable for Singapore haze salience, e.g. "haze" searches). [M]
- Tetlock, Paul C. (2011), "All the News That's Fit to Reprint: Do Investors React to Stale Information?", Review of Financial Studies 24(5): 1481-1512. DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhq141. Stale news causes overreaction among retail-heavy stocks. [M]
- Peng, Lin and Wei Xiong (2006), "Investor Attention, Overconfidence and Category Learning", Journal of Financial Economics 80(3): 563-602. DOI 10.1016/j.jfineco.2005.05.003. Limited attention theory. [M]
- Engelberg, Joseph E. and Christopher A. Parsons (2011), "The Causal Impact of Media in Financial Markets", Journal of Finance 66(1): 67-97. DOI 10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01626.x. Local newspaper coverage of the same event drives local trading; identification using weather/coverage variation. [M]
- Ben-Rephael, Azi, Zhi Da and Ryan D. Israelsen (2017), "It Depends on Where You Search: Institutional Investor Attention and Underreaction to News", Review of Financial Studies 30(9): 3009-3047. DOI 10.1093/rfs/hhx051. Bloomberg-terminal-based institutional attention; underreaction when institutional attention is low. [M]
- Bordalo, Pedro, Nicola Gennaioli and Andrei Shleifer: "Salience Theory of Choice Under Risk", Quarterly Journal of Economics 127(3): 1243-1285 (2012); "Salience and Consumer Choice", Journal of Political Economy 121(5): 803-843 (2013); "Salience and Asset Prices", American Economic Review P&P 103(3): 623-628 (2013); also "Salience", Annual Review of Economics 14 (2022). Volume/pages from memory; which of these to cite depends on the point. [M]

### Inferences
- Project framing "fires are public but salient only via Singapore haze" maps most directly onto Huberman-Regev, Tetlock (stale news) and DellaVigna-Pollet/Hirshleifer-Lim-Teoh (attention-dependent underreaction). Da-Engelberg-Gao suggests Google Trends on "haze" as an in-sample salience proxy.

### Gaps
- None of these re-verified at source this session; check all [M] pages/DOIs.

## 7. Fire attribution (firms' defence hypothesis)

### Takeaway
Concession share of fires/emissions is substantial but not dominant and attribution is contested because of overlapping community claims; this supports a "fires start outside concessions" defence being partly credible.
